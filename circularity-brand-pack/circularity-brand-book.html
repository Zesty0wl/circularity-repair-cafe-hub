01 · Brand strategy

Who Circularity is

Circularity is a South Derbyshire community charity that creates spaces and places where people connect: to each other, to nature, and to themselves.

Through a community managed library hub, a monthly Repair Café, the Grow project, the Wellbeing Wagon, a podcast and a studio, Circularity helps people find new hobbies, meet new friends, learn for life and improve their community. The work is practical and local, but the idea behind it is bigger: moving from a culture of "Me" to a culture of "We".

The name carries three meanings at once, and the whole identity is built on them: the circular economy of repairing, reusing and growing; the cycles of nature the Grow project celebrates; and the circle of community that always has room for one more person.

Positioning statement

For people who want to belong and to make their corner of the world a little better, Circularity is the community charity that turns everyday acts of repairing, growing and gathering into real human connection.

Connection Circularity Community Me to We
The single thought: The circle is open. There is always space for you. Everything we design should feel like an invitation in, never a wall to get past.
02 · Personality & values

Warm, grounded, hopeful, together

If Circularity were a person, they would be the neighbour who puts the kettle on, knows how to fix your lamp, has seeds to spare, and genuinely wants to hear how you are.

Warm

Welcoming and unpretentious. A safe space, a cup of tea, a friendly face. We lower the barrier to taking part.

Grounded

Practical and hands-on. Real things, made and mended by real people in a real place. We do, we do not just talk.

Hopeful

Curious and optimistic. Lifelong learning, creativity and the belief that small actions add up to change.

Together

Collaborative and inclusive. Everyone has something to give and something to gain. Me becomes We.

03 · Voice & tone

We talk like a good neighbour

Plain, warm and welcoming. We speak as "we" and to "you". We ask questions, we invite, and we never talk down.

Principles

Lead with an invitation, not an instruction. Use short, plain sentences. Prefer everyday words over jargon. Be specific about the real thing on offer (a cup of tea, a seed, a mended kettle). Celebrate people by name and credit local contributors. Keep it encouraging and hopeful, never guilt-driven.

Signature lines

"What brings you here?"
"Changing minds from Me to We."
"Come along and have a chat."
"You do not need any experience, just come as you are."

Writing rule for all Circularity copy: never use em dashes. Use commas, colons, brackets or a new sentence instead. Keep punctuation as warm and plain as the words.
04 · The logo

The Open Circle

The mark is a circle drawn as an open ring, with a single dot resting in the opening. The circle is the cycle, the community and the circular economy. The opening is the invitation. The dot is you: the "Me" stepping into the "We", and the seed that grows.

Primary horizontal lockup · use this wherever space allows
Stacked lockup · for square and centred placements

Colourways

Duotone mark · default
Reversed white · on teal/pine/photos
Mono ink · single-colour print
App icon / favicon

Clear space & minimum size

Keep clear space around the logo equal to the diameter of the dot on all sides. Never crowd it with text or other marks. Minimum sizes: 24px for the mark on screen, 140px wide for the horizontal lockup, and 10mm high in print.

Do & do not

✓
Keep the opening and dot intact. They carry the whole idea.
✓
Use the reversed white version on busy or dark photos.
✓
Give the mark room to breathe.
✕
Do not close the ring, rotate it, or recolour the dot at random.
✕
Do not stretch, add shadows, gradients or outlines.
✕
Do not place the duotone mark on a clashing colour. Switch to reversed white instead.
Production note: the supplied lockup SVGs set the word "Circularity" in Fraunces with a serif fallback. For final master artwork, convert the wordmark to outlines so it renders identically everywhere. The mark itself is pure vector and needs no conversion.

Proportion & accessibility

The 60 / 25 / 15 rule

Roughly 60% paper and neutrals, 25% teal and pine, and 15% split between terracotta and sunflower as accents. Let the warm neutral breathe. The brights are seasoning, not the meal.

Tested contrast pairings

Ink on Paper 13:1, passes AAA. Use for all body text.
White on Forest Teal 6.3:1, passes AA. Safe for buttons and normal text.
Ink on Sunflower 8.4:1, passes AAA. Always pair sunflower with ink, never white.
White on Terracotta 3.6:1, large text and buttons only. Use ink for small text on terracotta.

08 · Graphic language & photography

The arc, the dots, the circle

Beyond the logo, the brand draws on three repeatable devices: the arc as a frame and divider, clusters of dots as people, and the circle as a window onto real life.

The arc

Crop the ring to underline headings, divide sections, or sweep across the foot of a page. Always end an arc with a dot.

Dot clusters

Groups of dots in brand colours stand for people gathering. Use as a light pattern or to mark community stats.

Circle windows

photo
photo

Mask photography into circles or soft lozenges, optionally ringed in a brand colour, so images echo the mark.

Photography direction

Real people in a real place, never sterile stock. Warm natural light, candid moments, and hands at work: mending, planting, pouring tea, turning pages. Lean into greens and earth tones so images sit happily beside the palette. Credit local photographers, as the current site does.

No: flat studio stock, cold blue tones, staged handshakes, empty corporate spaces.

Notes for the AI agent

Treat this document as the single source of truth. Use the design tokens verbatim. Default to Paper backgrounds with Ink text, Forest Teal as the primary action colour, and Fraunces for headlines over Mulish body. Keep everything pill-rounded. End decorative arcs with a dot. Mask photos into circles where it helps. And in every piece of writing, never use an em dash.

When in doubt, ask one question: does this feel like an open invitation in? If yes, it is on brand.
